How to read this

The narrow pale line is the network's prior. The solid bar is the share of completed simulations. PUCT balances exploring likely moves with the value learned from simulated positions. Illegal columns get zero prior and visits. Ties choose the leftmost column. Inspect does not place a piece.

Practice, measured
without the mythology.

240 self-play games. A 42 → 64 → policy/value network. Learning targets come from search visits and the final game result, with no expert games or hand-written positional scores.

60 games each, alternating the first player, fixed recorded seeds. Tactical means: take an immediate win, else block an immediate loss, else choose randomly. Search does much of the work: the untrained network already plays reasonably with it. These small fixed samples do not establish a reliable strength gain or an Elo rating.

The learning loop

PUCT exploration constant 1.5. 24 simulations per training move. Root Dirichlet noise α=0.3, mixed at 25%. Sample visit proportions for the first 10 plies, then choose the most visited move. Every 20 games: 120 AdamW updates on replayed positions, with horizontal reflection augmentation, policy cross-entropy plus outcome mean-square loss. Replay holds at most 12,000 positions.

Method reference: Silver et al., Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play. This small dense network is inspired by the policy/value + tree-search loop. It does not reproduce the paper's architecture, scale or performance.

What this cannot promise

Connect Four has been solved, but this model is not a solver. Small search budgets miss tactics. The network was trained on a tiny self-play sample and can be confidently wrong. More simulations mean more time, not perfect play.
